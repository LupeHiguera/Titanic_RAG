## Methodology

- **Gold set**: 30 queries hand-curated from Titanic primary sources, covering testimony from both the US Senate (1912) and British Wreck Commissioner's (1912) inquiries. Each query labels every witness *expected to have testified on the topic* — including both inquiries' name variants for the same person (e.g. "Charles Herbert Lightoller" and "Charles Lightoller" both count as the Lightoller witness).
- **Hit Rate @ 5**: query is a hit if ≥1 relevant witness appears in the top 5 retrieved chunks. This is the most user-facing metric — it reflects whether a typical user would see a relevant answer above the fold.
- **Recall @ K**: mean fraction of a query's relevant witnesses that appear in the top K. Penalizes the system for missing relevant witnesses when many testified on a topic. Lower than Hit Rate by design.
- **MRR**: 1 / rank of the first relevant witness. Rewards systems that rank the right answer near the top, not just somewhere in top-K.

## Limitations

- **Witness-level relevance, not chunk-level.** A query gets credit when any chunk from a relevant witness appears, even if that specific chunk isn't the best evidence. Chunk-level labels would be more rigorous but exponentially more expensive to build.
- **30 queries is small.** Statistical confidence is limited; this is an indicative measure, not a benchmark. A 200-query gold set would be the next step for real production use.
- **No human grader for fuzzy cases.** "Charles Lightoller testified on lifeboat 6" is unambiguous historically. "Did anyone hear the band" is hazier — the labeling reflects judgment calls.
